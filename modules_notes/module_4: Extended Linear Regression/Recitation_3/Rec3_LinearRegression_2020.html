College Dataset¶

Let's assume the true model is given by,

$$ \text{Graduation Rate} \approx \beta_0+\beta_1 X_{\text{Top25perc}}+\epsilon$$

Estimated model through linear regression,

$$ \hat{y_\text{Graduation Rate}} = \hat{\beta_0}+\hat{\beta_1} X_{\text{Top25perc}}$$

We now cover some important statistical terms such as $R^2$ and Residual Standard Error.

$$ \text{RSS (Residual Sum of Squares)} = \sum_{i = 1}^{N}(y_i-\hat{y_i})^2$$$$\text{RSE (Residual Standard Error)} = \sqrt{\frac{RSS}{DF}} = \sqrt{\frac{RSS}{n-p-1}} = \sqrt{\frac{RSS}{n-2}}$$

Where DF is the degrees of freedom

$$\text{TSS (Total Sum of Squares)} = \sum_{i = 1}^{N}(y_i-\mu)^2$$

Where $\mu$ is the average of $y$.

Now the famous $R^2$ is,

$$R^2 = \frac{TSS-RSS}{TSS}$$

#Programming the above formulas

Residual Standard Error (RSE) is important because this is the estimate of $\sigma$ when we assumed the error term $\epsilon$ to be normally distributed with $N(0,\sigma)$

Confidence Intervals¶

  • $\hat{\beta}$ are the mean estimates
  • Every estimate needs a confidence interval (Obtained from its mean and standard error)
  • Confidence Interval $$\mu \pm (z\times \text{Standard Error})$$

95% Confidence intervals¶

$\beta_0$:

$$42.3651 \pm (1.96 \times 1.621) = [39.18,45.54]$$

$\beta_1$:

$$0.4140 \pm (1.96 \times 0.027) = [0.36,0.46]$$

We may interpret this as, with a 95% probability the true value of $\beta_1$ is contained within the interval $[0.36,0.46]$.

Hypothesis Testing and p-values¶

  • Is $\hat{\beta_1}$ statistically significant?

Problem Formulation¶

  • Null Hypothesis
    • $H_0:$ No relationship between $X$ and $Y$
    • $H_0: \hat{\beta_1} = 0$
  • Alternative Hypothesis
    • $H_a:$ There is some relationship between $X$ and $Y$
    • $H_a: \hat{\beta_1} \neq 0$

Interpretation¶

  • A small p-value:
    • We reject the null hypothesis at a particular significance level
    • There is a relationship between Top25perc and Grad_Rate at a particular significance level
  • A large p-value
    • We fail to reject the null hypothesis at a particular significance level
    • There is no association between Top25perc and Grad_Rate at a particular significance level

We never accept the null hypothesis, we only fail to reject it

  • For every unit increase in Top10perc the graduation rate increases by 0.0.33% while holding Top25perc constant.

Hypothsis Testing¶

The Null hypothesis we test for,

  1. $H_0: \beta_1 = 0$
  2. $H_0: \beta_2 = 0$
  3. $H_0: \beta_1=\beta_2 = 0$

Looking at the pvalues we can easily test null hypothesis 1. ($\beta_1 = 0$) and 2. ($\beta_2 = 0$).

  • We reject the null hypthesis that $\beta_1 = 0$ at a 0.05 level. Because 0.05>1.11e-2

  • We reject the null hypothesis that $\beta_2 = 0$ at a 0.05 level. Because 0.05>>1.027e-6

Testing the null hypothesis for 3.($H_0: \beta_1=\beta_2 = 0$) is not as straight forward.

  • We first fit a new model setting $\beta_1=\beta_2 = 0$ which results in $\hat{y} = \beta_0$. We then perform a type 1 analysis of variance (Anova) test. As shown below:

One Hot Encoding is the practice of transforming categorical data to numerical data.¶

$$\hat{Y} = \hat{\beta_0}+\hat{\beta_1}X_{European}+\hat{\beta_2}X_{Japanese}$$
$$X_{\text{European}} = \begin{cases}1,& \text{if origin is European}\\ 0,& \text{if origin is Not European}\end{cases}$$$$X_{\text{Japanese}} = \begin{cases}1,& \text{if origin is Japanese}\\ 0,& \text{if origin is Not Japanese}\end{cases}$$
$$\hat{Y} = \hat{\beta_0}+\hat{\beta_1}X_{European}+\hat{\beta_2}X_{Japanese} = \begin{cases} \hat{\beta_0}&,\text{if American}\\ \hat{\beta_0}+\hat{\beta_1}&,\text{if European}\\ \hat{\beta_0}+\hat{\beta_2}&,\text{if Japanese} \end{cases}$$
